Review & Pay — tender & change

Three ways to redesign the payment half of the New Sale checkout, each adding an Amount received field and a live Change to return readout. The dead white space in the current screen is what each sample spends differently. Numbers shown: grand total QAR 36.00, customer hands over QAR 50.00.

Sample A

Tender Pad

The counter-POS answer. The empty space becomes an on-screen number pad, so cash is keyed with the thumb and the OS keyboard never covers the totals. Change appears the instant the tendered amount passes the total.

  • Ticket collapses to one tappable strip — items expand on demand.
  • Quick chips: Exact, and the two notes above the total.
  • Best when most sales are cash and speed matters.

Sample B

One Ledger Card

The least disruptive change: tip, method, totals and status stop being four floating blocks and become one card that reads top to bottom like a ledger. Received is an inline editable row; Change sits directly under it and the status becomes a slim ribbon at the card's foot instead of a big empty panel.

  • Keeps the full itemised receipt visible — no collapsing.
  • Uses the OS keyboard; least code to build.
  • Best when card & credit are as common as cash.

Sample C · chosen & built

Cash Drawer

✓ Shipped — review_pay_screen.dart + cash_tender_card.dart. Built with a short-tendered warning the mock doesn't show, and the itemised receipt folded in behind the hero.

No typing at all in the common case — the cashier taps the notes the customer actually handed over and they add up. A hero panel states the amount due in one glance; the gold band underneath states what goes back in the customer's hand.

  • Tapping 50 then 10 reads 60 received; long-press a note to subtract.
  • The keypad ↔ notes toggle keeps odd amounts possible.
  • Best for a busy cash counter with a fixed note set (QAR 1/5/10/50/100/500).
