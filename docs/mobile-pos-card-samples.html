New Sale · product card — three directions

Same catalog, three different card ideas. Each is shown with a photo and without one, the price now lives on the image, and the green + button is gone — the whole tile already adds to the cart, so an added item announces itself with a count badge and ring instead.

tap anywhere on the tile = add to cart ring + count badge = already in the cart

Sample A — Gallery Frame

price pill floats on the photo
A square photo crowned by a small frosted price pill, then a clean caption: name over code. The pill sits in the same corner whether or not there's a photo, so a mixed catalog still scans as one grid. Closest to what's on screen today — it just moves the money onto the image and drops the button, which buys back a whole row of tile height.

Sample B — Editorial Scrim

price sunk into the image
The photo darkens toward its base and the price is set inside that gradient in serif white with a gold currency mark — the way a menu prices a dish. Name and code share one tight line beneath. Without a photo the panel becomes a large monogram with a watermark icon, so empty tiles still look deliberate rather than like missing images.

Sample C — Frosted Slab

the tile is the photo
No caption area at all: the card is the image, with one frosted glass slab across the bottom holding the name, the code and the price. Tallest, most product-forward, and the densest at 4-up — but it leans hardest on photo quality, so the no-photo tile is the one to judge carefully.
